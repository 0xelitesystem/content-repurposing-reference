# A repeatable workflow

Repurposing works best as a routine you run on every pillar piece, not as an occasional afterthought. A repeatable workflow turns the multiplication of one idea into many pieces from a creative scramble into a process you can do reliably and even hand off. The goal is a sequence you follow each time, so distribution stops depending on inspiration and starts being something that just happens.

## Start by choosing what is worth a pillar

The workflow begins before creation, with deciding which ideas deserve to be a pillar. Not every thought warrants the full treatment and the chain of derivatives. The ideas worth it are the ones with enough substance to fill a full piece and enough breadth to yield several distinct derivatives, on topics central to what you do. Choosing these deliberately, rather than turning everything into a pillar, keeps your effort concentrated on ideas that will repay the distribution. A strong pillar pipeline starts with selecting fewer, better core ideas.

## Capture derivatives while making the pillar

As covered earlier, the cheapest time to set up derivatives is while making the pillar, with the spokes in mind. The workflow builds this in: as you create the pillar, note the points that will become standalone posts, structure it so clean clips can be pulled, and articulate the key arguments clearly enough to lift into a thread or email. This adds little to the pillar's creation and saves the much larger cost of mining a finished piece for derivatives that may not be there. By the time the pillar is done, you already know what will come out of it.

## Extract and adapt in a batch

Once the pillar is complete, extract the derivatives in a focused session rather than piecemeal over weeks. Pull the clips, draft the posts, write the thread, write the email, adapting each to its platform as you go. Batching this is efficient because you are deep in the material and can move between formats while the ideas are fresh, and it produces a complete set ready to schedule. Treating extraction as a defined step in the workflow, with the platforms and formats you target listed, makes it systematic: you work down the list, adapting each piece, rather than deciding ad hoc what to make.

## Schedule across time, not all at once

The derivatives do not all need to go out immediately. Spreading them across days and weeks extends the life of the pillar, keeps a steady presence without constant new creation, and gives each piece its own moment rather than competing with the others. A pillar published once can feed a distribution schedule for a while, which is much of the point: sustainable output from concentrated creation. Scheduling the derivatives over time, rather than dumping them at once, also lets you see which derivatives land and learn what to emphasize next time.

## Review what worked and feed it back

The final step closes the loop: look at which pillars and which derivatives performed, and let that shape the next cycle. If short clips from a certain kind of pillar consistently reach new people, make more of those. If a platform never rewards your derivatives no matter how you adapt them, stop spending effort there. The workflow improves each time you run it by paying attention to results, so that over months your repurposing gets sharper, concentrating on the pillar types and platforms that actually return reach. A process plus a feedback loop is what turns repurposing from a chore into a reliable engine.
