# Pillar and derivative formats

The structure that makes repurposing work is hub and spoke: one substantial pillar piece at the center, and many smaller derivative pieces radiating out from it, each drawn from a part of the pillar and shaped for a specific platform. The pillar holds the full idea; the derivatives carry pieces of it to where people are. Getting this structure right is what separates systematic repurposing from random cross-posting.

## The pillar piece

The pillar is your most complete, substantial treatment of an idea: a full video, a long-form essay, a detailed guide, an in-depth episode. It is where the idea exists in full, with the depth and nuance that a short format cannot hold. The pillar is worth real effort, because everything downstream draws from it, and its quality sets the ceiling for every derivative. A rich pillar yields many strong derivatives; a thin one yields thin derivatives. The pillar is also the thing you can point back to, the home of the full idea that the derivatives gesture toward.

## Derivatives carry pieces outward

Each derivative takes a part of the pillar, a single point, a section, a clip, a key insight, and reshapes it for one platform. A long video might yield several short clips, a written thread summarizing the argument, a set of single-point posts, an email to your list, and an image or two capturing a key idea. None of these is the whole pillar; each is a facet of it, sized and styled for where it will appear. The derivatives work because a part of a strong idea, well presented, is genuinely useful on its own, while also drawing interested people back to the full pillar.

## Plan derivatives from the start

The most efficient approach is to know, while making the pillar, what derivatives you intend to draw from it, so you can capture what each will need as you go. Knowing you will pull short clips shapes how you structure a video into self-contained segments. Knowing you will write a thread shapes how clearly you articulate the key points. This is far cheaper than mining a finished pillar for derivatives after the fact and discovering it does not yield clean pieces. Designing the pillar with its spokes in mind makes the whole set come together quickly.

## Each derivative should stand alone

A derivative has to make sense to someone who has not seen the pillar, because most people who encounter it will not have. A clip that only makes sense if you watched the full video, a post that assumes the context of the essay, fails for the new viewer who is exactly who repurposing is meant to reach. Each piece needs enough on its own to deliver value to a first-time encounter, while pointing toward the pillar for those who want more. The derivative is both a complete small thing and an invitation to the larger thing.

## The pillar earns its derivatives back

The relationship runs both ways. Derivatives carry the idea outward and bring new people back to the pillar, which is usually the piece that does the deeper work of building trust or driving an action. A short clip might reach someone new; the full video is where they decide you are worth following. Treating the derivatives as the top of the funnel and the pillar as where the depth lives gives the whole structure a purpose beyond reach: the spokes find people, and the hub converts the interest into a real connection. This is the same hub-and-spoke logic that the internal-linking reference applies to a website, here applied to content across platforms.
