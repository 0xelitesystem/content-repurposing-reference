# Adapting to each platform

The most common repurposing mistake is copying a piece across platforms unchanged: posting a video link everywhere, pasting an essay as a thread, sharing the same caption on every network. It fails because each platform has its own format, audience expectations, and way of rewarding content, and a piece shaped for one looks out of place on another. Adapting, rather than copying, is what makes a derivative work where it lives.

## Each platform rewards different things

Platforms differ in what they show people and why. Some favor short, punchy, native content and bury anything that sends people away. Some reward depth and discussion. Some are built around search and reward content that answers a clear question. Some are inbox-based and personal. A piece that thrives on one platform can sink on another not because the idea is weaker but because the format and the platform's incentives are different. Understanding what each platform actually rewards, native content over outbound links, short over long or the reverse, is the basis for adapting rather than guessing.

## Match the format, not just the topic

Adapting means reshaping the piece into the platform's native format, not just changing the wrapper. A point from a video becomes, on a short-video platform, a self-contained clip with its own hook; on a text platform, a written post that makes the point in prose; in an email, a personal note to a known reader. The underlying idea is the same; the form is rebuilt each time. Pasting the video's description as a text post, or linking out where the platform punishes outbound links, ignores the format and the piece underperforms. The work of adaptation is real but small relative to creating the idea.

## Respect the audience's expectations

The same person expects different things in different places. A reader who wants depth in their inbox wants brevity in a feed. A follower who tolerates a hard call to action in one context finds it jarring in another. Adapting includes matching the tone and the ask to what the audience expects on that platform: more personal and direct in email, more concise and self-contained in a feed, more thorough where depth is welcome. Carrying the same tone and the same ask everywhere ignores that the audience's mindset shifts with the platform, and content that misreads the mindset feels off even when the idea is good.

## Native beats outbound, mostly

A recurring tension is that platforms generally reward content that keeps people on the platform and suppress content that sends them away, while creators want to drive people to their pillar or their own site. The resolution is usually to deliver real, complete value natively, so the platform rewards the piece, while making the path to more available for those who want it, rather than relying on the link as the whole point. A clip that is genuinely useful on its own, with a route to the full thing for the interested, outperforms a clip that is just a teaser for an outbound link the platform will bury. Give value where people are, and invite rather than redirect.

## Adaptation is the difference between reach and noise

The line between repurposing that builds an audience and repurposing that produces noise is whether each piece was adapted or merely copied. Adapted derivatives feel native, deliver value in the platform's own terms, and respect the audience there, so they perform and build presence. Copied content feels lazy and out of place, performs poorly, and can make a creator look like they are spraying the same thing everywhere without care. The extra effort of adapting each derivative to its home is what turns one idea into genuine reach rather than into the same square peg jammed into many round holes.
