# What not to repurpose

Repurposing is a multiplier, and multiplying the wrong thing produces more of the wrong thing. Used without judgment, it generates exactly the thin, repetitive, low-value content that the no-slop principle exists to avoid. Knowing what not to repurpose, and when not to repurpose at all, is as important as the technique, because the failure mode of repurposing is flooding every platform with hollow filler.

## Do not multiply a weak idea

The first rule is that repurposing amplifies whatever it starts with, so a weak pillar yields weak derivatives, just more of them. If an idea was not strong enough to be worth one good piece, splitting it across ten formats does not improve it; it spreads thinness wider. The discipline is to repurpose only pillars with genuine substance, and to let weak ideas stay small or unpublished rather than inflating them into a distribution campaign. More pieces of a hollow idea is not reach; it is noise that makes your whole output feel thinner.

## Do not pad to hit a quota

A trap creators fall into is deciding they must produce a fixed number of derivatives from every pillar, then padding to reach it, stretching a piece that honestly yields three good derivatives into eight by adding filler. The padded pieces have nothing real to say and dilute the strong ones. Let the pillar yield however many genuine derivatives it actually contains, and stop there. A pillar that produces three strong standalone pieces should produce three, not eight including five that exist only to hit a number. Quotas push toward slop; substance should set the count.

## Do not copy where you should adapt

As covered earlier, copying a piece across platforms unchanged is a failure, but it deserves restating as a thing not to do, because it is the most common lazy shortcut. If you are not going to adapt a derivative to its platform, do not post it there. A piece pasted across networks without reshaping performs poorly and signals carelessness. It is better to skip a platform than to dump unadapted content on it. The choice is adapt or abstain, never copy, because copied content costs you credibility for no reach in return.

## Do not repurpose the genuinely time-bound or context-bound

Some content does not travel. A piece tied tightly to a moment, a reaction to a passing event, a time-sensitive announcement, loses its point once the moment passes, and dragging it across platforms later lands flat. Content deeply dependent on a specific context, where removing it from that context strips the meaning, also resists repurposing into standalone derivatives. Recognizing which pieces are inherently of-the-moment or context-locked saves you from forcing them into formats where they no longer make sense. Not everything is a pillar, and not every pillar yields portable derivatives.

## Do not let distribution outrun substance

The deepest version of the principle: repurposing should never become an end in itself that drives you to create more pillars than you have real things to say, just to feed the distribution machine. When the workflow starts demanding pillars faster than genuine ideas arrive, the temptation is to manufacture pillars from thin material to keep the spokes turning, and the whole output degrades into volume without value. The right rhythm is to let substance set the pace: create pillars when you have something worth the full treatment, repurpose those widely, and accept quieter stretches over filling them with hollow content. Reach built on substance compounds; reach built on slop erodes the trust that made the audience worth reaching.
